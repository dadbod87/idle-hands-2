# Useful play while long jobs run

November 11, 2026. Nick explicitly asked for enough optional activity during long production and for research into comparable games. This plan extends the accepted local neighborhood independently of the still-unapproved hosting gate. **Targets and future systems below are design decisions, not claims of implemented content or measured retention.**

## What the reference games teach us

These are developer descriptions and official support material, checked November 11. They establish mechanics, not proof that one mechanic caused commercial success. No source code, art, dialogue or exact balance values were imported.

| Reference and primary source | Described pattern | Our adaptation / boundary |
|---|---|---|
| [Stardew Valley — developer overview](https://www.stardewvalley.net/about/) | Several skills, recipes, exploration, relationships, restoration and home expression develop alongside one another. | A player needs personal, social and exploration goals while production runs. Skills should open choices and recipes rather than multiply every source of output. Do not copy its game-day assumptions into real-hour jobs. |
| [Graveyard Keeper — developer store description](https://store.steampowered.com/app/599140/Graveyard_Keeper/) | Gathering, crafting, quests, other ventures and dungeon exploration connect to the management game. | Use chains that make a material or service useful in several places, with humor in the task. Show where ingredients come from and what unlocks next; avoid opaque prerequisite chains. Its content and tone are not a template for our office jokes. |
| [Farm Together 2 — developer store description](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2418520/Farm_Together_2/) | Production advances offline; town requests, decorating, expansions and house activities provide different reasons to play. | Keep long work safe offline and offer creative/social/active alternatives. A workbench must not reserve the entire player's ability to progress. Do not add attendance chores or price waiting relief. |
| [Palia — developer skills article](https://www.palia.com/news/the-skills-of-palia) and [official gameplay support](https://support.palia.com/hc/en-us/articles/7475603096980-Gameplay) | Practice opens recipes/tools, skills supply other activities, and furnishing/gardening/cooking/exploration coexist. | Separate a garden/workshop/field activity lane; let resources connect them without making all lanes compete for the same exhausted starter material. Start with one well-tested lane before a large skill list. |

Our inference is **parallel goals with different verbs and time horizons**. A bigger list of timers is not more playable depth. Optional active play should widen options and expression; someone returning tomorrow should retain a useful path rather than discover their crops died.

## IH2-062: first independent activity

The town sketchbook is a small observation chapter at three existing square trees. Walk or tap a visible tree, read its clue, choose an observation, and save an illustrated page. Every tree has a text alternative; weather, bridge funding, home ownership and a busy bench never block it. A completed book reveals one illustrated journal keepsake, once. It costs no resources and awards no XP, money, kit, furnishings or production bonus.

This seeds a collection/exploration lane without changing the economy. It remains a **short, finite chapter**, not enough content for an entire twelve-hour wait. Current jobs preserve their exact start/ready timestamps; ordinary elapsed depot settlement remains unchanged. Legacy saves gain an empty sketchbook, preserving their existing property and progress. Shared completion still requires validated proximity and exact transactional retry receipts. Local browser saves retain their existing single-writer protections.

## Next playable layers, in order

1. **Garden and practical gathering prototype:** one small starter garden, explicit plant/harvest verbs and varied growth-stage art; choose a small crop catalog and outputs before implementation. Give it an independent capacity from the bench. Ready plants wait; optional tending may improve convenience or appearance, never make absence destructive. Model starter seed access and renewable seed/output loops so the garden cannot consume the last scrap needed for orientation or become an infinite instant currency source.
2. **Useful town requests and services:** a few authored multi-step errands with distinct actions, an ingredient source you can actually reach, transparent recipes, and persistent unlocks. Keep a request board useful when every production slot is full. Never promise an unopened shop as today's destination.
3. **Expression and mastery:** more original furnishing families, separate clothing slots, finish-preserving customization and skill-specific tools. A new tier should add a new decision or recognizable object, not just a larger number or a reskinned old item.
4. **Cooperation and outings:** contribution choices, safe resource help, authorized shared activity, then optional wilderness/PvE after the identity and recovery gates. Avoid a missing coworker blocking the whole town. Chat and private messages retain their separate identity/audience gates.

Broader region or economy work gets its own bounded task and evidence. This order is a reversible agent-selected design choice based on the references and the actual gap, not a claim of completed gardening, fishing, combat or shops.

## Pacing and acceptance contract

Design targets: a two-to-five-minute check-in can finish one useful action and leave a clear next goal; a deliberate ten-to-twenty-minute session should offer at least two different active verbs and one creative or social choice while the bench stays busy. These are **future playtest targets**, not current measured play duration.

Before inviting players, exercise fresh town/building bridge/open bridge/exhausted depot/full bag/busy bench/ready bench/late newcomer states. For each, list actions actually executable now, paid prerequisites, expected next unlock and safe logout. A proposed lane fails if every useful action depends on the same busy slot or the same exhausted material. Keep a visible 'while work runs' route and truthful ready/blocked states; no forced minute-by-minute checklist.

Simulation should compare brief daily, regular and highly active schedules over several weeks. Record meaningful unlocks, scarce ingredients, blocked time, overflow, optional collection progress and housing milestones. Active play can earn variety, convenience and bounded progress; extra check-ins must not compound all output. Tune real durations only after those paths and phone interactions are observed. Keep slow long-term progression; avoid both empty waiting and an infinite click economy.

## Core identity: funny, social, personally worthwhile

Nick reinforced these as core requirements on November11: humor, teamwork, useful activity, solo progress, and visible homes/acquired/equipped items. Judge each major content slice against all five over time, rather than pretending one tiny chapter supplies them all.

- Humor belongs in the purpose, description and consequence of a task: an overqualified stump, a committee-approved tool, a café waiting for permission to boil water. Keep instructions and prices clear; an amusing failure must still tell the player what happened.
- Teamwork should produce a shared change everyone can see and leave a useful entry point for newcomers. Stagger contribution choices, show who helped, avoid a single final-click winner, and keep solo alternatives where coordinated attendance would block progression.
- Personal mastery and possessions remain persistent. Earn new tools/recipes/appearance choices through distinct activities, not a universal multiplier or a weekly wipe. Protect homes and valuable collections from friendly mischief.
- Visible expression needs recognizable silhouettes and base motifs, visitable homes and deliberate display/equip choices. Plan a public display system for earned journal discoveries and trophies; do not imply the current journal-only Naturalist keepsake already appears on a house wall. Keep private inventory and personal chat separate from chosen public displays.
- A cooperative player and a solo player should both find a useful next action. Group projects, visits and later shared practical activities complement an individual's garden, workshop, collection, quests and decoration.

After the garden/field loop, the next expressive slice should let an earned collection item be deliberately displayed at home, with owner/visitor checks, a visible source of the item, preserved art identity and safe return to storage. Wardrobe/tool layers follow the existing frame/foot-anchor art contract; no generic recolor should erase the item someone earned.

### Avoid repeating the early access gap

Agent-selected next-slice default: the first garden should be a personal rented allotment available during orientation, not require earning the twelve-kit house first. Keep bed ownership separate from shared scenery; preserve it through later property changes. Test seed access and storage with a0scrap/1timber/3supplies bag and an occupied bench before choosing prices.

Visible progress should also have an early opt-in outlet before expensive housing: a small chosen public display or resident showcase is a candidate. Do not silently expose the private rental workshop or the whole inventory. Later earned homes provide the fuller expression path. Likewise, a newly arrived coworker needs a useful group role after the initial bridge is already funded; repeating the same one-off upkeep forever is not the intended social game.

Beyond the first ten-to-twenty-minute acceptance path, a future thirty-to-sixty-minute voluntary session should have several destinations, active gathering/practical verbs, more than one quest choice and creative/social options. This remains a content/playtest target. Finite tutorial cards and a collection checklist alone do not meet it. No incentive should require staying online for a twelve-hour manufacturing job.
