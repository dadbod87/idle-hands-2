# First serving gesture — Aster art study

One clear action should have one short visual response. This candidate explores the future confirmed café handover without adding a crafting queue, an edible inventory item or another menu. The plate is illustrative: its two painted slices are not a quantity or a promise about a recipe.

The first rig is Aster. Four facing directions each have ready, reach, serve, return and rest poses on a 64×80 frame. The foot remains at 32,72. The final resting body is byte-identical to the accepted original rig, and the lower foot pixels remain identical throughout the gesture. Recovery tucks the hand back and removes the plate. The west body is an exact vector mirror of east. North preserves the back of the head and presents the plate to its side.

The separate gallery defaults to rest at the actual 0.9 display scale. Replay shows one proposed 720 ms sequence and returns to rest; it never loops automatically. Changing direction or motion preference, or hiding the document, cancels the sequence. Still inspection is explicitly available even when reduced motion is selected. Enlarging is a review tool, not a change to game scale. There is no gameplay or animation-state integration, destination, item grant or room authority.

Terra’s first bounded anatomy/menu review found the profile connection too subtle. A thin original ink outline now separates the supporting sleeve from the torso. The second review found that connection clearer at game size and 2×, with no sampled new blocker. Native contrast captures composite the existing grass and wood-floor artwork behind the study; these are DOM art composites, not actual Phaser world scenes.

The browser raster check decodes each frame in an expanded 80×96 canvas, so it detects rather than crops away stray alpha. All 20 cells fit their actual frame. Exact same-facing foot RGBA equality passes. East/west vector geometry is identical under reflection; cross-transform browser antialias fringes differ, so their raw RGBA differences are diagnostics and mirrored raster bounds allow a one-pixel fringe. This does not loosen any actual-frame crop or foot assertion.
