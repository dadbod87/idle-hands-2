# Show the ground destination while walking

Tap-to-walk gives players a route but provides little confirmation about the spot they selected. Draw one small static chalk mark at the last point of the accepted route. It stays on the ground as the camera follows, under the actor and town props, and disappears when walking ends, changes room, yields to manual input, or locks behind a menu. Never draw a confirmation for an unreachable point. The mark is intentionally small to keep the world clear. For a named destination outside the current view, it becomes visible only when the camera reaches that ground.

The cue follows existing paths without altering how taps are interpreted, the collision grid, position, movement acknowledgements, speed or persistence. It creates one graphics object per scene and has no repeating animation, new asset fetch or server request. Existing labels and keyboard/touch movement remain the primary controls.

Local build and one focused actual-game browser scenario passed on desktop, narrow phone and short 200% landscape. The test compares arrival against the exact displayed world target and checks redirection, keyboard takeover, and menu cancellation. A touch at 200% can land slightly away from the test's screen-point estimate after resizing, so the test distinguishes the intended approximate tap zone from the exact accepted path. No intercepted data, original-game state, API or protected services are involved. Terra's stills found the marker discernible and unobtrusive, with the broad desktop framing making it especially subtle. Short captures and DOM checks do not establish physical touch behavior or contrast on every terrain.
