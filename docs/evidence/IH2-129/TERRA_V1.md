# Audio audition study: visual review

Reviewed desktop, phone390, phone360, phone320, phone200, phone320-200, short and short200 captures. The selected place is explicit, the three choices are distinct, and “Play music” is visually prominent beside “Mute all.” The compact town buildings, tree, paper palette and committee humor fit the established office-town visual language. “Music preview · not in game” communicates the study's scope.

No obvious clipping or visual blocker is apparent in these full-page captures. Normal phone layouts retain readable labels and large buttons; enlarged views reflow places and sound buttons into fewer columns. At phone360 the “Little sounds” label and 25% value sit very close together, and phone320 wraps the percentage onto a separate line. A little more separation or stacking that slider label and value would make the volume easier to scan. At phone320-200 the “Little sounds” label and 25% also touch visually. This is a small layout polish opportunity, not proof of an unusable control.

At enlarged narrow sizes the place cards precede the play action by a substantial vertical distance, although the long page itself is expected. A compact place-card height at enlarged narrow widths could bring playback closer while retaining readable labels and generous touch targets. The stills do not establish viewport scroll position, scrolling accessibility, focus, or control operation.

The operator separately reports focused browser interaction, bounds and Axe checks. This is a visual-only review of a standalone study, not game integration, CI/main acceptance, physical-device validation, or a listening or music-quality assessment; hardware output was muted.
